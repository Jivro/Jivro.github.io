Avijit Chowdhury

I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Indian Institute of Astrophysics (IIA), Bengaluru. My research investigates how dark matter, accretion flows, plasma, and other astrophysical environments modify black hole dynamics and observable signatures. I develop analytical and computational models of perturbations, accretion, tidal response, and ringdown, aiming to connect strong-field gravity theory with multi-messenger observations.

Research Interests

My research uses compact objects as laboratories for strong-field gravity. I investigate how dark matter, accretion flows and plasma shape gravitational-wave and electromagnetic signals, and how these environmental effects can be distinguished from departures from General Relativity.

Black Hole Ringdown and Dark Matter

I study how dark matter halos modify quasinormal modes, light-ring properties and late-time tails. I also combine ringdown spectra with tidal Love numbers to break degeneracies between halo profiles that appear similar in ringdown alone.

Relativistic Accretion and Dark Matter

I investigate how dark matter halos alter transonic accretion onto supermassive black holes. My work shows that halo properties affect the critical-point structure and can enhance disc luminosity, particularly for compact, massive halos.

Tidal Response and Horizon Diagnostics

I study tidal Love numbers of compact objects in matter-rich environments. My work finds that the logarithmic dependence of scalar and spin-1 tidal response on compactness can survive the presence of a self-gravitating relativistic thin disc.

Modified Gravity and Compact Objects

I examine perturbations and radiation in theories beyond Einstein gravity, including quadratic and Lovelock gravity. This work clarifies when the eikonal quasinormal-mode/light-ring correspondence fails and how scalar hair changes ringdown, stability and Hawking emission.
