Selected Research Contributions

Compact Objects, Their Environments, and the Observable Signatures of Gravity

My work connects gravitational-wave signatures with accretion and radiation from compact objects. The central question is how to distinguish the influence of surrounding matter from genuine departures from General Relativity.

1. Dark Matter Imprints on Black Hole Ringdown

I modelled a dark matter halo as an anisotropic Einstein cluster with a generalised (α, β, γ) density profile, derived the axial perturbation equation, and calculated quasinormal modes using sixth-order WKB and direct time-domain evolution.

Key result

The halo redshifts both the oscillation frequency and damping rate. For centrally concentrated halos, the inner density slope is the dominant profile parameter; the outer parameters are subleading.

Physical interpretation

In the eikonal limit, the shift is captured by a redshift integral over halo mass outside the light ring, explaining the connection between quasinormal modes, light-ring frequency and Lyapunov exponent.

Time-domain evolutions confirm the WKB spectrum, recover the late-time power-law decay and show that the intermediate tail is controlled by the outer density falloff.

2. Breaking Degeneracies with Tidal Response

Different halo compactnesses and density profiles can produce nearly indistinguishable ringdown spectra. A ringdown measurement alone may therefore be insufficient to infer the surrounding matter distribution.

Main result

The static axial tidal Love number samples the halo with a different radial weighting from ringdown. Combining tidal response and quasinormal modes can separate configurations that either observable leaves degenerate, offering a way to distinguish environmental effects from departures of the vacuum geometry.

3. Accretion as an Electromagnetic Probe of Dark Matter

I studied relativistic, low-angular-momentum, inviscid advective accretion onto a supermassive black hole embedded in a cold dark matter halo.

  • The halo changes the critical-point structure and topology of the transonic flow.
  • It enhances disc luminosity by an amount determined by halo mass and compactness.
  • For compact and massive halos, the inner flow dominates the luminosity contribution, making emission a potential probe of the central dark matter spike.

This provides an electromagnetic complement to the gravitational-wave constraints from ringdown and tidal response.

4. Radiation Transport and Horizonless Compact Objects

I derived the dynamics of electromagnetic fields in cold accreting plasma around static, spherically symmetric horizonless compact objects.

Plasma quasibound states

The complex frequencies oscillate with plasma frequency. Their oscillation amplitude is set by surface reflectivity, while the oscillation length scale depends on compactness. The plasma can therefore carry information about the object's surface.

Tidal response in a disc

The logarithmic dependence of scalar and spin-1 tidal response on compactness, arising from near-horizon physics, persists when the object is embedded in a self-gravitating relativistic thin disc.

5. Testing Gravity Beyond Einstein's Theory

Quadratic gravity

I showed that the massive spin-2 mode carries more gravitational-wave energy than the massive spin-0 mode and quantified the backreaction of the emitted radiation on the horizon, with implications for detectability by LISA.

Lovelock gravity

I demonstrated that the eikonal correspondence between quasinormal modes and unstable null circular geodesics can fail generically. Relating the spectrum to the causal structure of the field equations through polarisation-dependent effective metrics restores the appropriate interpretation.

Scalar hair and exotic compact objects

My earlier work showed that scalar hair modifies quasinormal spectra and superradiant stability, and changes Hawking emission, greybody factors and emission sparsity. I also established axial stability and distinctive ringdown echoes for the Janis–Newman–Winicour spacetime, while finding the studied four-dimensional Einstein–Gauss–Bonnet configuration to be unstable.

6. Connecting the Messengers

These results motivate a unified approach in which gravitational-wave ringdown, tidal response and electromagnetic emission constrain the same environmental model. Each observable samples the surrounding matter differently. Used together, they can help separate the properties of the central compact object from the influence of its dark matter halo, accretion flow and plasma.

Current Directions

I am extending the joint ringdown and tidal framework to rotating backgrounds and inspiral signals, developing more realistic accretion models with viscosity and magnetic fields, and investigating environmental effects on extreme mass-ratio inspirals. A long-term goal is to incorporate these effects consistently into waveform models for future gravitational-wave observations, including LISA.
